# Photos (no app, no LiDAR, no depth)

**Phone:** iPhone 15 or newer. **App:** the built-in Camera app — nothing to install.

## Settings
- Open Camera, swipe to **Photo** mode (not Pano, not Portrait).
- Use the **1x** lens (the plain middle one — avoid the 0.5x "ultra-wide" button, it bends straight lines near the edges).
- Flash **off**. Turn the room lights on instead.
- Hold the phone **sideways (landscape)**, the same way every time.
- Hold it roughly **level** — imagine the top and bottom edges of the phone are a shelf; keep that shelf flat, not tilted up at the ceiling or down at the floor.
- Stand still, aim, then tap the shutter. Do not walk or swing the phone while it fires.

## One folder per room
Create one folder per room (name it anything: "kitchen", "bedroom_1", ...). Each folder will hold that room's photos only — except the doorway photos below, which get copied into two folders on purpose.

## For every room: 5–6 photos (never fewer than 2, never more than 8)
1. Walk to roughly the middle of the room.
2. Turn slowly on the spot. Every quarter turn or so (about every 60–90 degrees), stop and take one photo.
3. Each photo should overlap the one before it by about a third of the frame — if the last photo's right edge is still visible on the left edge of this one, that's about right.
4. Try to get all the walls of the room into one photo or another, with a bit of floor and a bit of ceiling line visible in each.
5. Skip mirrors and dark rooms where you can't see detail; turn on a light or open a curtain first.

## For every doorway, open archway, or hallway mouth connecting two rooms (this is the important part)
This is what lets the computer figure out which rooms are next to each other — skipping it means the rooms cannot be stitched together.

1. Walk up to the doorway and stand right in the middle of it (one foot could almost be in each room).
2. Without moving your feet, take one photo facing into the first room.
3. Turn around on the same spot and take one more photo facing into the second room.
4. You now have two "doorway photos." Put a **copy of both** into **both** rooms' folders (so each of the two rooms ends up with its own photos *plus* these same two doorway photos). It is fine that one of the two doorway photos doesn't really belong to a room visually — copy it in anyway.
5. Do this for every doorway, hallway, or open archway in the home, including ones that lead to a room you are not photographing (skip only closets).

## Handing off
- Give the pipeline the parent folder containing all the room sub-folders.
- JPG, HEIC, or PNG are all fine, straight off the phone — do not screenshot, edit, or crop them.
- 2 to 8 images per room folder (after adding any doorway copies).

## Avoid
- Panorama mode, Portrait mode, Live Photos used as stills, or any "AI" cleanup/filters.
- Walking or turning while the shutter is open (blur).
- Photos through glass, mirrors, or straight into bright windows/lights.
- Extreme close-ups of a single wall with nothing else in frame.

If a step above is unclear in the moment, do the simplest literal reading of it — the pipeline is built to work with whatever that produces, intervals widen honestly when coverage is thin.
